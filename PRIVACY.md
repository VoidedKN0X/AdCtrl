# Privacy

AdCtrl is built so that your DNS data stays between your phone and your own AdGuard Home server.

- **Only your servers.** AdCtrl talks only to the AdGuard Home servers you add. Every other destination is blocked inside the app. There are no third-party SDKs, ads or analytics making network calls, and the app never contacts Google.
- **Telemetry-free.** No accounts, analytics, usage statistics, crash reporting or tracking of any kind.
- **Your query log stays private.** The query log shows what everyone on your network visits. It's only ever sent between your phone and your own server, and AdCtrl never writes it, your passwords or addresses containing your passwords to the system log, and never stores the query log on the phone.
- **Last known Overview.** To show the Overview when your server can't be reached, AdCtrl keeps the latest statistics (including top domains and clients) in its private storage. It's never backed up or sent anywhere, and is deleted when you remove the server.
- **Passwords are encrypted.** Server passwords are stored encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore, which never leaves the phone. With the app lock on, they're also sealed with a key that only exists while AdCtrl is unlocked, unless you turn on background checks (which have to sign in while AdCtrl is locked).
- **App lock.** The app PIN is never stored, only used to unlock a key; fingerprint unlock uses a key in the phone's secure hardware. While AdCtrl is locked, nothing is sent to your servers except the background checks you turned on.
- **Notifications and the widget.** Background checks (off until you turn them on) and the home screen widget only read from your own AdGuard Home servers; they never change anything. What they last saw (protection state, the last 24 hours' query counts, which problems were notified) is kept in AdCtrl's private storage and never backed up. On the lock screen, notifications only say that something needs a look, without server names or details. While the app lock has your passwords sealed, the widget only says that AdCtrl is locked.
- **Push through ntfy (optional).** Off until you turn it on. AdCtrl registers only with the ntfy app on your phone, which gives it a push address on your own ntfy server; AdCtrl never connects to that server itself. A push only starts the usual background check (at most once a minute); its content is never shown, stored or used. Only broadcasts carrying the secret token of AdCtrl's registration are accepted, and turning push off removes the registration.
- **Nothing is backed up.** AdCtrl's data is excluded from Android backups and from moving to a new phone.
- **Secure connections.** HTTPS by default. Plain HTTP is only possible for addresses on your own network, per server, after a warning. A self-signed certificate is accepted only after you confirm its SHA-256 fingerprint, and only for that server.
- **Screenshots and recent apps.** **Hide in screenshots** and **Hide in recent apps** (Settings › Privacy and security, both off by default) blank AdCtrl in screenshots and screen recordings, and in the recent apps view. Server setup, the lock screen and the PIN screens are always kept out of screenshots, and with the app lock on the recent apps view is always blank.
- **Links.** The Support, Report a bug, Source code and Licence rows open in your browser only when you tap them.

AdCtrl uses network access, and the standard Android permissions for scheduled background work (so checks can run after a restart). The only permission it asks you for is showing notifications, when you turn on background checks.
